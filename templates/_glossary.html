What do these words mean?
Phase
One of six stages: discover the idea, validate it, build a first version, set up the offer, launch, and learn.
Checkpoint (gate)
A pause at the end of each phase. The system compares the evidence with targets it wrote down before the work, so the result cannot be argued into a yes. It then advises: go on, repeat, rethink, or stop.
Rethink (pivot)
Keep the goal but change something basic, such as the customer, the problem or the offer.
Kill criterion
A fact that, if true, means the idea should not continue. For example: nobody pays for anything like it.
Verified claim
A statement with a web source whose exact words were checked on the page. Anything without that is shown as an ASSUMPTION.
ASSUMPTION
Something we believe but have not proven. It is always labelled.
Demand test and ratio
A small test of interest, such as sign-ups. The ratio is what you got divided by your target: 0.12 means 12 out of 100. Below 0.2 the advice is to stop; 0.5 or more is a go.
MOCKED, SAMPLE, SCRIPTED
Practice data, not real. MOCKED is simulated, SAMPLE is example data, SCRIPTED means the answer came from a demo file instead of a person.
Override
You disagree with the system's advice and choose differently. Your choice is saved next to the advice.
